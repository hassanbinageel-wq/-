What is multiple pregnancy?

When more than one fetus simultaneously develops in the uterus at the same time (twins, triplets, quadruplets, quintuplets, etc.), it is called multiple pregnancy.

Most common

Twins

2 fetuses

Triplets

Quadruplets

Quintuplets

Simultaneous development of two fetuses (twins) is the most common.

Diagnosis · A. History

Family history of twinning

More often present on the maternal side.

Ovulation-inducing drugs

History of use, especially gonadotrophins, for infertility.

Exaggerated symptoms of normal pregnancy

i · Early pregnancy

Increased nausea and vomiting in the early months.

ii · Later pregnancy

Cardiorespiratory embarrassment, such as palpitations or shortness of breath.

iii · Venous symptoms
  • Swelling of the legs
  • Varicose veins
  • Hemorrhoids
iv · Abdominal & fetal
  • Unusually rapid abdominal enlargement
  • Excessive fetal movements, may be noticed by an experienced parous mother

B. Examination

1. General examination

  • Anemia is more prevalent than in singleton pregnancy.
  • Unusual weight gain, not explained by preeclampsia or obesity, is an important feature.
  • Evidence of preeclampsia is a common association 25 %

2. Abdominal examination

Inspection

  • Abdominal size is increased compared with the gestational age (GA).

Palpation

  • Fundal level is higher than expected for the calculated GA.
  • Three or more fetal poles may be palpable after 24 weeks.
  • Fetal movements may be felt over the whole abdomen.
  • Too many fetal parts may be palpable.

Auscultation

  • Two fetal heartbeats may be heard with different intensities.

3. During labor · per-vaginal (PV) examination

Two bags of forewaters

A small presenting part with an oversized abdomen

Cephalic on vaginal examination while abdominal examination suggests breech

Ultrasound is essential for diagnosis.

C. Ultrasound examination

Transabdominal ultrasound (TAS) is the gold standard for diagnosing multiple fetal pregnancy.

Its uses include

  • Estimation of the number of fetuses and the true gestational age.
  • Determination of zygosity (MZ or DZ) and chorionicity (MC or DC).
  • Diagnosis of congenital anomalies (FCA), conjoined twins, polyhydramnios and discordant twins.
  • Evaluation of fetal wellbeing for each fetus using: Biophysical profileDoppler
  • Diagnosis of the second twin’s presentation: VertexBreechTransverse
  • Diagnosis of fetal and maternal complications.
  • Routine investigation of pregnancy.

D. Diagnosis of chorionicity

Chorionicity is best diagnosed by ultrasound at 10–13 weeks of gestation.

2. Hospitalization

Routine hospitalization

  • Admission solely for bed rest is not considered essential.
  • Bed rest at home from 24 weeks onward: physical and mental rest, improves uteroplacental circulation.

Benefits of bed rest

↑ Birth weight of the babies↓ Frequency of preeclampsia↑ Prolongation of pregnancy

Prevention of preterm delivery

  • Routine betamimetics or cervical cerclage: no significant benefit.
  • Corticosteroids to accelerate fetal lung maturation for women with preterm labor before 34 weeks.
Twins develop pulmonary maturity 3–4 weeks earlier than singletons.

Emergency hospitalization

Development of complicating factors necessitates urgent admission, irrespective of the period of gestation.

B. Management during delivery · 1. First stage

Conducted according to the usual principles for singleton pregnancy, with additional precautions:

Skilled obstetricianpresent
Experienced anesthetistavailable
Neonatologistspresent
Ultrasoundin labor ward is helpful
  • Patient should remain in bed to prevent early rupture of membranes.
  • Analgesic drugs used cautiously: babies may be small and rapid delivery may occur.
  • Epidural analgesia is preferred: facilitates manipulation of the second fetus if necessary.

Additional precautions

  1. Careful fetal monitoring.
  2. Internal examination soon after rupture of membranes to exclude cord prolapse.
  3. Establish an IV line with Ringer’s solution for urgent IV therapy if needed.
  4. Keep one unit of compatible, cross-matched blood readily available.
  5. Ensure that a neonatologist is present at delivery.

D. Mode of delivery

E. Delivery of the first baby

Same general principles as normal labor, with the following additional points:

  • Liberal episiotomy under local infiltration with 1 % lignocaine.
  • If forceps delivery is needed: preferably under pudendal block anesthesia.
  • General anesthesia is better avoided: the second baby may be affected by prolonged anesthesia.
  • Do not administer IV ergometrine with the delivery of the first baby.
  • Clamp the umbilical cord at two places and cut between them.
  • Leave at least 8–10 cm of cord for medication or transfusion if required.
  • Hand the baby to the nurse after labeling it as number 1.

B. Discordant twins

Definition

One fetus shows abnormally increased growth, while the second fetus is small for gestational age (SGA), with a difference in fetal weight of more than 25 %.

Causes

  1. Placental insufficiency
  2. Intrauterine growth restriction (IUGR)
  3. Structural anomalies occurring in one fetus
  4. Twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome (TTTS)

C. Locked twins

Definition & mechanism

Most common: the after-coming head of the first baby becomes locked with the fore-coming head of the second baby.

Another situation: first twin breech, second cephalic or transverse: the heads lock at the pelvic brim during vaginal delivery, preventing engagement of either head.

Diagnosis

  • Keep the possibility in mind during labor.
  • Confirmed by intrapartum sonography or radiography.

Management

  1. Vaginal manipulation to separate the chins of the fetuses.
  2. If this fails: cesarean section is necessary.
  3. Destructive procedure: decapitation of the first baby if already dead, delivery of the second baby, then removal of the decapitated head. historical description

D. Vanishing twin syndrome

Definition

Early in the first trimester, one embryo dies and subsequently disappears, while the second continues to grow in a normal pattern.

  • Serial ultrasound in multiple pregnancy may reveal the death of one fetus with continuation of pregnancy in the surviving fetus.
  • If the dead fetus is lost within 14 weeks, it may simply “vanish” through resorption.

Rate of disappearance

up to 40%

E. Conjoined twins

Extremely rare

Definition

Twins that are born with their bodies physically connected.

Clinical features suggesting conjoined twins

  • Failure of traction to deliver the first twin during the second stage.
  • Inability to move one twin without moving the other.
  • Obstruction during the second stage of labor.

Diagnosis

  • Often diagnosed during delivery when obstruction occurs in the second stage.
  • A bridge of tissue between the fetuses on vaginal examination confirms the diagnosis.
